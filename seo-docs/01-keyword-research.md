# Keyword Research Rules — xtoolkit.live

## The Core Rule

**Never write an article before checking who ranks for that keyword.**
A keyword with zero competition for small sites is worth 10x more than a high-volume keyword owned by Moz, HubSpot, or Neil Patel.

---

## Step 1 — The 3-Question Filter (Do This Before Writing Anything)

For every keyword candidate, ask these 3 questions in order. Fail any one → skip the keyword.

### Q1: Who is on page 1?
Google the exact keyword phrase. Look at the first 5 organic results (ignore ads).

**Green light — proceed if you see:**
- Small niche blogs or tool sites (anything you've never heard of)
- Forums (Reddit, Quora, StackOverflow)
- Small SaaS tools in the email/dev/SEO niche
- Sites with domain names you don't recognize

**Red light — skip if you see:**
- Neil Patel, Moz, Ahrefs, Backlinko, HubSpot, Semrush Blog
- Google Developers, MDN, W3Schools
- Wikipedia on the first result
- Major media (Forbes, TechCrunch, Wired, Lifehacker)
- Big brands (Norton, LastPass, Microsoft, Apple, Cloudflare)

### Q2: Does the search intent match what we can deliver?
Google classifies search intent into 4 types. We can win on:
- ✅ **Informational** — "what is X", "how to do X", "X explained"
- ✅ **Tool-seeking** — "free X tool", "X generator online", "X checker"
- ✅ **Comparison** — "X vs Y", "best X for Y"
- ❌ **Navigational** — Someone searching for a specific brand (e.g. "gmail login") — we'll never win
- ❌ **Transactional/commercial** — "buy X", "X pricing" — dominated by e-commerce and SaaS

**Also check format:** Look at what Google shows on page 1. Is it how-to articles? Lists? Tool pages? Write the same format Google already rewards.

### Q3: Can we link this to one of our tools?
Every article must link to exactly one xtoolkit.live tool naturally. If the topic has no connection to any of our 44 tools, it's off-topic — skip it.

---

## Step 2 — Long-Tail vs Short-Tail

### Short-tail (1–3 words)
Examples: "email signature", "JSON formatter", "password generator"

**Reality check:** Short-tail keywords have massive volume but massive competition. A new site has near-zero chance of ranking for "email signature" when HubSpot, WiseStamp, and Exclaimer spend millions on SEO.

**Rule: Never target a short-tail keyword as the primary keyword.** Use short-tail terms inside the article body as supporting mentions only.

### Long-tail (4+ words)
Examples: "email subject line spam words to avoid", "how to check if twitter account is suspended", "free email signature generator no signup"

**Why long-tail wins for us:**
- Lower competition — fewer authoritative sites bother writing about hyper-specific topics
- Higher purchase/conversion intent — someone searching 6 words knows exactly what they want
- Easier to rank — one well-written page can land on page 1 within 2–3 months

**Rule: Every article must target a primary keyword of 4 words minimum.** Ideally 5–7 words.

---

## Step 3 — Finding Keywords We Can Win

### Method 1: Google Autocomplete
Type the start of a topic into Google and watch what autocomplete suggests. These are real phrases people type.

Example:
- Type "how to check twitter account" → autocomplete shows "how to check twitter account is suspended", "how to check twitter account without logging in", etc.
- These autocomplete suggestions are GOLD — they have real search volume and are specific enough to be winnable.

### Method 2: People Also Ask (PAA)
Search for any broad topic related to our tools. Scroll to the "People Also Ask" box. Every question there is a real keyword.

Example: Search "temp mail" → PAA shows:
- "Is temp mail safe to use?" ← 4 words, winnable
- "Can you use temp mail for Gmail?" ← 6 words, very specific, very winnable
- "What is a temporary email address?" ← broad, may be competitive

### Method 3: "vs" and "alternative" keywords
These have commercial intent and lower competition:
- "[our tool] alternative"
- "[competitor] vs [our approach]"
- "best free [tool type] online"

### Method 4: Tool-specific "how to" queries
Every tool on our site has a set of "how do I actually use this" questions people search:
- "how to format tweets for X"
- "how to bulk check twitter accounts free"
- "how to generate a url slug for WordPress"

These are almost always winnable because only sites with that specific tool write about them.

## Step 4 — Keyword Difficulty by Site Age

xtoolkit.live is less than 1 year old with limited backlinks. Apply this filter:

| Keyword type | Can we rank? | Timeframe |
|---|---|---|
| Very specific long-tail (5–7 words) | ✅ Yes | 1–3 months |
| Specific long-tail (4–5 words), niche SERP | ✅ Yes | 2–4 months |
| Medium-tail (3–4 words), niche SERP | ⚠️ Maybe | 4–8 months |
| Any keyword with Neil Patel/Moz/Ahrefs on page 1 | ❌ No | Never |
| Short-tail (1–3 words) | ❌ No | Never (for now) |

As the site gains authority (backlinks, indexed pages, traffic), revisit medium-tail keywords.

---

## Step 5 — Keyword Placement in the Article

Once you have your target keyword, place it here (in priority order):

1. **SEO title** — keyword within the first 5 words ideally
2. **URL slug** — 3–5 words, keyword included, no stop words
3. **H1 / article headline** — same as or slight variation of SEO title
4. **First 100 words** — natural mention within the opening paragraph
5. **At least one H2 heading** — use the keyword or a close variation
6. **Meta description** — include keyword naturally, add a benefit hook

**Natural density target:** 1–2% of total word count. For a 1,200-word article, the primary keyword should appear 12–24 times across all forms (exact match + variations + synonyms).

## Keywords Permanently Blocked for xtoolkit.live (Too Competitive)

These keyword categories will never be winnable at our current authority level:

- Anything with Neil Patel ranking
- "how to create/build/make [anything]" as a 3-word phrase
- "what is [fundamental concept]" when that concept is a CS/SEO basic (e.g. "what is SSL", "what is DNS")
- "best [tool category]" lists (HubSpot, G2, Capterra dominate)
- "free online [tool]" for any tool category with HubSpot/Google offering one
- Anything health, legal, finance adjacent (YMYL — dominated by certified sources)
